Category, Subcategory & Competitor Review

Our Kids · 8 categories, 13 trading subcategories, 4,000 traded SKUs · trailing 30 days to 24 Aug 2026 · benchmarked against the demand we can measure

How to read this. Categories come from Odoo (revenue, gross profit, inventory). Subcategories are built by classifying the 4,000 SKUs that took a product view in the last 30 days, because neither Odoo nor Shopify holds a usable subcategory field — that is itself a finding. Traffic is Egypt only. The metric that ranks everything here is GPPV, gross profit per product view, using each category's own margin rate rather than a blended one. Site average GPPV is 1.88 EGP. Competitor benchmarking uses our own auction and search data; we hold no third-party market-share source for Egypt, and the last section says plainly what that costs us.

What the categories are telling us

32% of views · 0.48% conversion

Backpacks and school sets are the largest traffic sink in the business

308,931 views, 39.8% of online revenue, and the worst conversion of any major line. 37.8% of those views land on a product with zero sellable stock. This is not a demand problem or a price problem — it is an availability problem that we are paying Google and Meta to walk into, in the one month of the year when the demand is guaranteed.

3.68% conversion on 3.9% of views

Stationery is the best-converting line in the store and we barely show it

27.6% add-to-cart, 3.68% conversion, 214 SKUs. It converts eight times better than school bags. Its GPPV is low only because the average order is 122 EGP — which makes it the obvious basket-builder, not a standalone destination. Attaching stationery to every school-bag page is the single cheapest conversion fix in this deck.

8,445 EGP average order · 0.15% conversion

Strollers and car seats earn the most per view and get 1.9% of the traffic

The highest GPPV in the range at 3.28 EGP a view, on 82 SKUs and 17,979 views. Google shows 82,655 impressions of stroller and car-seat demand in 30 days converting at 0.50%. A category with an 8,445 EGP basket does not convert on a product page alone — it needs comparison, fitting, warranty and finance content, none of which the site has.

0.68 EGP per view

Clothing is 25% of the company and the worst-performing thing on the website

0.68 EGP of gross profit a view, 0.58% conversion, 3.4% of views — against 42% of in-store revenue and 58% of the inventory. Clothing is not failing as a business; it is failing as a website category. It is 90% listed, so this is a merchandising, imagery and navigation problem, not a listing one.

20.6% of views sold out

Bikes, scooters and ride-ons are a real category we treat as an accessory

3,719 EGP average order, 2.66 EGP a view, and 74 SKUs. Google shows 38,170 impressions of bike and scooter demand at a 21.7× return on 1,294 EGP of spend — one of the best returns in the account, on a rounding-error budget.

1,840 SKUs, 3.5% of revenue

Half the traded catalogue earns almost nothing

260 SKUs make half of online revenue and 776 make 80%. Below that, 1,676 SKUs took views in 30 days and sold nothing at all — 197,000 views, 20.5% of all product views, spent on products nobody buys. They are not free: they consume search relevance, category pages and photography time.

Structural

Nobody owns subcategory in either system

Odoo has eight categories, six of which are duplicated as “(Consignment)”. Shopify has eight product types, plus Arabic duplicates and three typos. Neither holds subcategory. Every subcategory number in this deck was reconstructed by classifying product names, which is defensible for a diagnosis and useless as an operating system. Until a subcategory field exists and is enforced, no buyer can be held to a range plan.

Where we stand against the competition

Brand defence, not growth

A quarter of our search budget buys back our own name

21,257 EGP of 84,103 EGP of measurable search spend went on people typing “our kids”, at a 43.7× return. That number is not performance, it is a receipt for demand we already had. Strip it out and the rest of search returns 11.1× — still good, but a fifth of the story it appears to tell.

74% of non-brand search spend

Three quarters of our non-brand search spend produced nothing at all

15,975 non-brand search terms took 46,637 EGP and 20,365 clicks for zero conversions. The largest single line items are our own brands — “b z bags”, “glossy bird”, “smiggle bags” — landing on out-of-stock pages. This is not wasted spend to be negated away; it is stock and merchandising failure showing up on the media invoice.

The rivals we can see

We appear on competitor searches and convert almost none of them

Hedeya, Bingo, Kidilo, Jansport, Owala and Migo together took 1,053 EGP of clicks in 30 days at a blended 0.9% conversion; Jansport alone took 471 EGP across 11,361 impressions for zero orders. Either we carry an answer to those brands and are not showing it, or we should not be bidding. Both are decisions, and neither has been made.

Blind spot

We have no third-party read on the Egyptian market at all

Merchant Center best-sellers and competitive-visibility return no data for Egypt. We have no Similarweb, no panel, no price-scraping. Everything in this section is our own auction data, which tells us what we lose but never what the winner got. For a business of 522M EGP that is a real gap, and it is cheap to close: a monthly price and assortment scrape of five named rivals would cost less than one week of Meta spend.

The category action plan

Now

Stop paying for traffic to sold-out pages

37.8% of school-bag views and 20.6% of ride-on views land on zero-stock SKUs. Automate a daily feed check: any SKU with zero sellable stock comes out of Shopping, out of PMax and off the collection page, and its ad traffic redirects to the nearest in-stock sibling. Owner: e-commerce ops + performance. Measure: sold-out view share below 10%.

Weeks 1–4

Re-point traffic from school bags to the lines that earn per view

Move view share from Backpacks (32%, 2.29 EGP a view) toward Strollers & car seats (3.28), Swim (2.72) and Bikes (2.66), and attach Stationery to every school page as a basket-builder. Owner: e-commerce merchandising. Measure: site GPPV from 1.88 to 2.40.

Weeks 2–8

Give Clothing a website, not a listing

Clothing is 42% of the shops and 1.3% of online gross profit. It needs size guides, model photography, filterable age and gender navigation, and outfit merchandising — the things that make apparel convert. Owner: category buying + e-commerce. Measure: Clothing conversion from 0.58% to 1.2%, GPPV from 0.68 to 1.20.

Quarter

Build the high-basket categories a proper journey

Strollers, car seats and nursery carry 8,445 and 479 EGP baskets and convert at 0.98–0.15%. They need comparison tables, safety and fitting content, in-store fitting appointments booked online, and instalment payment at the top of the page. Owner: e-commerce + retail. Measure: 1,200 EGP-plus band conversion from 0.56% to 1.0%.

Quarter · structural

Create a subcategory field and enforce it in both systems

One taxonomy, owned by buying, written to Odoo and mapped to Shopify product_type and to the Merchant Center feed. Without it, none of the numbers on this page can be reproduced next month by anyone but the person who built them. Owner: buying + IT. Measure: 100% of traded SKUs carrying a subcategory.
