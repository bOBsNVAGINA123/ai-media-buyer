The Demand Gap

Our Kids · the demand we already own, already pay for, and do not convert — 16,643 Google search terms, 399,679 on-site searches and 1.03M product views, 30 days to 24 Aug 2026

What this deck is, and what it reverses. The obvious next move for a retailer with a 14% online share is to buy more traffic or open more categories. This deck says do neither yet. Every measurable pocket of demand in this business is already reaching us — through Google, through Meta, and through 399,679 searches a month on our own site — and we already carry live range against almost all of it. The problem is not that we cannot find customers. It is that we lose them after they arrive: to sold-out pages, to a cart that leaks 85%, to a search box that cannot read Arabic, and to a price cliff at 1,200 EGP. Fixing capture is worth more than any new category, and it costs no capital. Where range genuinely is thin — car seats, strollers, footwear — the last section shows how to widen it on consignment, which is a model this business already runs at a third of its revenue.

Three scenarios, not one forecast

EGP millions of online revenue per month. Each step is arithmetic on a measured gap, and each is independently achievable.

How each step is sized

Sold-out pages: 191,350 views on zero-stock SKUs × each subcategory's own conversion and average order value. Cart→checkout: today 15.2% of carts reach checkout against a 30% norm for this basket size; the two steps split that gap in half so the middle case stands on its own. Paid search: bringing each theme's click-to-conversion up to the 1.30% account average on today's click volume, at today's average order value. Nothing here assumes more traffic, more budget or more stock.

Why we lose it — five mechanisms, all fixable

Mechanism 1 · 1.62M EGP a month

We sell shelves we know are empty

607 products with 191,350 views have zero sellable stock. In Backpacks and school sets it is 37.8% of all views; in ride-ons 20.6%; in swim 17.9%. The worst offenders are our own brands — Glossy Bird and Smiggle sets at 2,200–4,300 EGP — and we are actively bidding on their names in Google while their pages say sold out. A daily feed rule that pulls zero-stock SKUs out of Shopping, PMax and collection pages fixes this in a sprint.

Mechanism 2 · 6.54M EGP a month

Eighty-five percent of carts never reach the checkout

110,878 add-to-carts produced 16,803 checkouts. Add-to-cart rate is healthy at 10.7% of views — customers want the product. The loss is entirely in one step, on mobile, where 98.6% of Egyptian traffic sits. Until someone instruments that step we are guessing between delivery cost, payment method, login friction and shipping-time disclosure. It is the most valuable unknown in the company.

Mechanism 3 · the price cliff

Conversion collapses above 1,200 EGP and 55% of revenue lives there

Under 200 EGP converts at 4.45%; over 2,500 EGP at 0.26%. The add-to-cart rate holds up far better than conversion does, which means the objection is not the product — it is what happens after the cart at that price. Instalments, cash-on-delivery clarity and delivery cost shown before the cart are the levers, not discounting.

Mechanism 4 · language

Our search box does not speak the language our customers search in

“لانش بوكس” returns nothing, and 237 sessions a month type it. So does “stainless steel box” — 2,710 sessions — while the Smash and Sistema stainless boxes are among our best-selling products. /search is the most-visited page on the site with 399,679 views, ahead of the homepage. A synonym file and Arabic normalisation is a week of work against the single highest-traffic page we own.

Mechanism 5 · range depth

Only two themes are genuinely thin, and both are high-basket

Car seats and strollers: 117 live SKUs against 82,655 impressions of demand, an 8,445 EGP basket and the highest profit per view in the range at 3.28 EGP. Kids footwear: 484 live SKUs against 25,763 impressions and 738 on-site searches a month, converting at 0.58%. Everywhere else we already carry live range — 714 live SKUs in feeding, 591 in stationery, 448 in swim. Depth is not the constraint. These two are the exception, and neither needs to be bought.

The three ways to enter, side by side

On a 100 EGP sale, and on the cash it takes to get there.

Owned stock earns most per sale and is the only model that ties up cash and carries markdown risk. Consignment earns 8 points less and costs nothing. Agency — the vendor ships, we take a fee — earns least and is the only model that lets us list a category before we have decided to be in it.

Car seats & strollers

Widen on consignment before committing a pound of stock

82,655 impressions of demand a month, 0.50% conversion, 117 live SKUs, 8,445 EGP average basket, 3.28 EGP of gross profit per view — the highest in the range. We already stock Joie and Chicco and already run a Joie shopping campaign that converted 9.9 times in 30 days on 8,092 EGP. Add depth on consignment terms with the same vendors, build the comparison, safety and fitting content the category needs, and offer in-store fitting booked online. If it works, buy it. If it does not, we return it.

Kids footwear

The one genuine category gap, and it belongs in the branches first

“school shoes”, “كوتشي اطفال” and “صنادل اطفال” show 25,763 Google impressions and 738 on-site searches a month against 484 live SKUs converting at 0.58%. Footwear is a fit-and-return category, which is exactly what seven branches are for. Test it consigned in two doors alongside the school range, list what sells, and let the in-store return route carry the risk that kills footwear e-commerce elsewhere.

The two things that can kill this

Risk 1

Stock accuracy is the whole plan

Everything here — listing the gap, stopping sold-out traffic, consigned depth — assumes Shopify knows what is on the shelf. Today 607 live products show zero stock and still take 191,350 views, which means the signal exists and nothing acts on it. If we publish 4,854 more SKUs on top of an inventory feed nobody trusts, we will convert a merchandising problem into a customer-service one. Fix the feed rule first, in the same sprint, not after.

Risk 2

Consignment margin discipline

Consignment runs 19% gross margin against 27% owned. At a third of revenue that is already costing roughly 2.7 points of blended margin, and it is invisible because nobody reports it. Widening consignment without a floor on terms trades cash efficiency for margin we cannot afford to keep giving away. Set a minimum consigned margin by category before the next vendor conversation, and report consigned versus owned margin monthly.

The action plan

Sprint 1 · worth 1.62M EGP a month

Stop paying to send people to empty shelves

Daily rule: any SKU with zero sellable stock leaves Shopping, PMax and the collection pages, and its inbound ad traffic redirects to the nearest in-stock sibling. Restock or delist the 607. Owner: e-commerce ops + performance. Measure: sold-out view share from 20.5% to under 10%.

Sprint 1–3 · worth up to 6.54M EGP a month

Instrument the cart, then fix the one step that loses 85%

Full mobile funnel instrumentation on cart and checkout, then work the top three drop reasons. This is the largest number in the deck and the least understood. Owner: e-commerce. Measure: cart→checkout from 15.2% to 22% by day 45 and 30% by day 90.

Week 1 · cheapest fix in the deck

Teach the search box Arabic and synonyms

Arabic normalisation, a synonym file covering “box / lunch box / لانش بوكس”, “flask / bottle / فلاسك”, “mofakera / mofkera”, and a zero-results report that goes to merchandising weekly. Owner: e-commerce. Measure: zero-result searches below 5% of site searches.

Weeks 4–12 · zero capital

Widen car seats, strollers and footwear on consignment

Same vendors, consignment terms, agreed margin floor. Build the comparison and fitting content, book in-store fitting from the product page. Owner: buying + e-commerce. Measure: car-seat and stroller conversion from 0.15% to 0.6%, footwear from 0.58% to 1.0%.

What I need from you

Four decisions

1. Do we agree that capture comes before category? This deck argues against opening anything new until the cart and the stock feed are fixed. If we would rather grow range, say so now, because it changes every priority below.

2. Who owns the cart investigation, and by when? 6.54M EGP a month sits behind one unmeasured step. I want a named owner and a date, not a backlog ticket.

3. Do we set a consigned margin floor? Consignment is 33% of revenue at 19% margin and nobody reports it separately. I want a floor by category and a monthly consigned-versus-owned margin line.

4. Do we commit to the car seat and stroller category properly, or stop bidding on it? Today we spend 4,529 EGP a month on stroller and car-seat search for 7.2 conversions. Either it gets the range, content and fitting service it needs, or we stop buying the traffic. Half-in is the most expensive option on the table.
