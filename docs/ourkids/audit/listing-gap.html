The Listing Gap

Our Kids · what the seven shops sell that the website cannot · 18,756 trading SKUs matched barcode-to-barcode against 71,944 Shopify variants · 30 days to 24 Aug 2026

The method, so you can argue with it. Every product that sold in a branch in the last 30 days was pulled from Odoo with its barcode, its category, its stock on hand, its cost, and the date it first entered the system. Every one of the 37,681 products and 71,944 variants in Shopify was pulled by barcode and SKU with its status and publication date. The two were joined on barcode — not on name — and each in-store SKU was labelled live, in Shopify but never published, or not in Shopify at all. The prize figure assumes an unlisted SKU, once listed, earns online at the same rate as an already-listed SKU of the same business: 23.6 EGP online for every 100 EGP it earns in store, which is the measured ratio for the 13,902 SKUs that are already live. The twelve findings

13.1M EGP a month

25.7% of what the branches sell has no live product page

4,854 of 18,756 trading SKUs. Not a long tail: these SKUs did 13.1M EGP in 30 days, an average of 2,700 EGP each, and they include eleven of the twenty best-selling school sets in the company.

2,138 SKUs · 8.75M EGP

Half the gap is products that are already in Shopify and were never published

They exist, they have a barcode, a title and a price. Somebody built them and nobody pressed publish. That is 17.1% of in-store revenue behind a single toggle.

School, in August

The worst-covered category is the one in season

School listing coverage is 67.4% by revenue. 714 school SKUs doing 6.63M EGP a month in the shops are not on the site — in the single month of the year when school demand is guaranteed, and while we spend 16,575 EGP on Google searches for school bags.

1,960 SKUs · 9.4M EGP/30d

The newer the stock, the less likely it is to be online

Every SKU first stocked in the last 120 days that has never gone live. The buying team is landing range faster than merchandising can publish it, and the newest range is the range with the most demand behind it.

6.97M EGP of stock

2,611 of these SKUs are sitting in a warehouse right now

In stock, sellable, unlisted. Listing them costs a merchandiser's time and nothing else, and it is worth 1.49M EGP a month of online revenue.

Party 63.7% · Toys 63.9%

Party and Toys are the two structurally worst-listed categories

Both sit around 64% coverage. Party also has the worst GMROI in the business at 0.65× and the highest gross margin at 37.7% — a high-margin category we are neither stocking well nor selling online.

2,642 SKUs

A tenth of the gap has never been created in Shopify at all

4.33M EGP a month of in-store revenue from products that have no Shopify record of any kind. These need photography and copy, not a toggle — which is why they need to be scheduled, not just approved.

607 products · 191,350 views

Listing is only half the problem — a fifth of views hit sold-out pages

37.8% of school-bag views and 20.6% of ride-on views land on products with zero sellable stock, led by our own Glossy Bird and Smiggle sets at 2,200–4,300 EGP. We are paying to send people to shelves we know are empty.

2,710 sessions

“stainless steel box” returns nothing, and it is our best-selling product

The Smash and Sistema stainless steel snack boxes are among the highest-revenue SKUs on the site. The search box cannot find them because the titles say “Snack Box” and the customer types “box”. This is a synonym file, not a project.

Arabic

The site search does not work in Arabic, and 40% of our paid demand is Arabic

“لانش بوكس” returns nothing. Arabic terms make up a large share of every Google demand theme in this business — شنط مدارس, مايوه اطفال, كوتشي اطفال — and every one of them lands on a search box that only speaks English.

444 + 294 sessions

“school shoes” and “shoes” are asked for on our own site and answered thinly

Footwear shows 25,763 Google impressions and 738 on-site searches a month against 93 live products. Kids' footwear is the clearest genuine range gap in the business, and it is a category the branches could test on consignment before a single box is bought.

Zero capital

None of this requires buying anything

Waves 1 and 2 are stock we already own. Waves 3 and 4 are reorders of proven sellers, not bets. The entire 10.0M EGP a year of gross profit in this deck comes from selling what we already sell, to people who are already on our website, asking for it by name.

The plan, by wave

Week 1 · 1,053 SKUs · 3.2M EGP GP a year

Wave 1 — publish the new arrivals that are already in stock

Highest revenue, freshest demand, stock on the shelf, and 3.92M EGP of inventory currently doing nothing online. Most are already built in Shopify and need publishing, not creating. Owner: e-commerce merchandising. Done by end of week 1.

Weeks 2–4 · 1,558 SKUs · 1.6M EGP GP a year

Wave 2 — publish the rest of the in-stock range

Older range, still selling, still in stock. Lower revenue each but 3.05M EGP of inventory attached. Batch by category, School first. Owner: e-commerce merchandising.

Weeks 2–6 · 907 SKUs · 4.0M EGP GP a year

Wave 3 — reorder and list the new range that has sold out

The biggest single prize in the deck and the one that needs the buying team. These are products under 120 days old that sold 5.17M EGP in 30 days and have no stock left. Reorder, then list on arrival, not six weeks later. Owner: buying + merchandising.

Ongoing

Wave 4 and the standing rule — nothing enters stock without a listing

The remaining 1,336 older SKUs are a judgement call, category by category. The rule that stops this recurring is not a wave: no goods-received line closes in Odoo until the SKU has a Shopify record, and a weekly exception report goes to the same person every Monday. Owner: buying + IT.

What I need from you

Three decisions

1. Do we publish everything we stock, by default? Today the default is not to. I want the default reversed: everything we buy goes online unless someone gives a reason. The only real objection is stock accuracy, and that is a settings question, not a strategy one.

2. Who owns publish-on-arrival, and do they have the headcount? 4,854 SKUs is real work — 2,642 of them need photography and copy from scratch. I would rather be told the number is one person and it will take a quarter than be told yes and watch it not happen.

3. Do we fix search and stock signals before we spend another pound on traffic? A fifth of the views we already pay for land on sold-out pages, and our own search box cannot find our best-selling product or read Arabic. Buying more traffic before fixing that is buying a bigger leak.
