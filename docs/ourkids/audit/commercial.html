Our Kids — Commercial Diagnostic

13-month trend to 24 Aug 2026 · 7 branches + ourkids-eg.com + marketplaces · Odoo ⋅ GA4 ⋅ Shopify ⋅ Google Ads · prepared for the founders and the e-commerce team

Read this first — four rules this deck follows. (1) August 2026 is month-to-date (24 of 31 days). Anything labelled RR is run-rated ×1.29 so it compares like-for-like; ratios (MER, conversion, GPPV, share) are unaffected. (2) All web traffic is Egypt-only. 11.2% of raw GA4 sessions come from Singapore, the UK, India and Romania and buy nothing — including them understated every conversion rate by about a tenth. (3) Every revenue and margin figure has a gross and a net-of-returns-and-exchanges reading. In-store returns are already netted inside the POS data (5.6% of gross); online refunds are not, and run at 10.2%. Use the toggle on the MER and GPPV charts. (4) GPPV = gross profit per visit — online gross profit ÷ Egypt sessions. It is the metric this deck is built around, because revenue per visit hides the fact that we buy expensive traffic for cheap-margin categories.

Why company MER is not the number to manage

Company MER (all revenue ÷ all media) is 21.6×, because 83% of revenue walks into a shop. That number will look healthy no matter how badly the site performs. Manage online MER and GPPV; use company MER only to check that total media never exceeds its share of total gross profit — currently 15.2%.

Where the cash sits — by vendor

Vendor is the supplier/brand recorded against each product in Odoo (related_vendor_id). 354 vendors carry stock or revenue; the 72 above 1.5M EGP of trailing-twelve-month revenue are shown here and the complete file is exported alongside this deck. GMROI = trailing-twelve-month gross profit ÷ the owned inventory we hold at cost. Below 1.0× a vendor destroys cash. Consigned stock is excluded from the denominator because we have not paid for it — 35.8M EGP of the 99.0M EGP sitting in our warehouses is not ours, which is also why warehouse stock exceeds the 66.2M EGP on the balance sheet. Last received and the L7 / L30 / L90 / L365 columns come from goods actually booked in from a supplier location (98,334 receipt lines, 246.2M EGP at cost in twelve months). Intake index = a vendor's share of its category's last-90-day intake ÷ its share of that category's gross profit. Above 1.0 we are buying it harder than it earns; below 1.0 we are starving it.

Buying discipline

Nobody sees GMROI at the moment the order is placed

The four largest owned positions — Playmore 9.68M, Ourkids Alnass 4.9M, I.Wear 4.05M, Al Sayad 3.0M — return 0.55×, 2.7×, 1.11× and 2.0×. There is no pattern, because the number does not exist on the buying sheet. Put GMROI, months of cover and the intake index on the order form and most of this deck stops recurring.

What is working — protect it

Protect

Retail execution is the strongest thing in the company

Seven doors, every one of them up year on year, and revenue per door up 30% to 7.92M EGP a month even while opening a seventh. Mall of Arabia went from nothing in October to the second-biggest door in ten months. Whatever the store teams are doing, it is working, and nothing in this deck should be allowed to disturb it.

Protect

We are not a discount business

Online discounts are 1.6% of gross sales and in-store discounts 1.7%. Blended gross margin has held between 23% and 27% for thirteen months. Unlike most retailers of this size, growth here has not been bought with price. That is a genuine asset and it means the margin problems in this deck are fixable without touching price architecture.

Protect

Google Ads is the only paid channel that pays for itself online

93,146 visits at 2.44 EGP each, converting at 1.18% and returning 4.90 EGP of gross profit per visit. It earns twice what it costs and it is receiving one tenth of the media budget. It is also the channel most directly tied to demand we already own — 25% of its search spend is people typing our own name.

Protect

Consignment is already a third of the business and it costs no cash

Consignment runs 33% of in-store revenue and 20% online, at roughly 19% gross margin against 27% on owned stock. Lower rate, zero working capital, zero markdown risk. It is the cheapest way to test any new category and we already have the vendor relationships and the process.

The gaps — and what each one is worth

Worth ~3.1M EGP a month of online revenue

A quarter of in-store revenue is not listed online

13.1M EGP of trailing-30-day branch revenue, across 4,854 SKUs, belongs to products with no live Shopify listing. 2,138 of those SKUs exist in Shopify as ACTIVE but were never published. School is the worst offender at 67.4% coverage — in the middle of back-to-school. Listed SKUs earn 23.6 EGP online for every 100 EGP they earn in store; applied to the unlisted 13.1M that is 3.1M EGP a month of online revenue nobody has to buy any stock to get.

Worth ~2.9M EGP a month

85% of add-to-carts never reach the checkout

110,878 add-to-cart events produced 16,803 checkouts and 5,649 orders. Add-to-cart rate is fine. The collapse is entirely between cart and checkout, and it has got worse as traffic has grown. Recovering that step to a normal 30% would add roughly 5,000 orders a month at the current 1,610 EGP average order value.

Costing ~1.0M EGP a month of gross profit

Meta paid traffic loses money on every visit it delivers

439,584 visits at 4.89 EGP each returning 2.58 EGP of online gross profit. That is minus 2.31 EGP per visit, or about 1.02M EGP a month of online gross profit, before a single box is packed. It may be repaid by store visits — but that has never been measured, and a channel taking 90% of the media budget on an unmeasured assumption is the largest open risk in the company.

1.88M EGP of demand landing on nothing

One in five product views lands on a page that is out of stock

607 products with 191,350 views in thirty days show zero sellable inventory on Shopify. The list is led by our own brands — Glossy Bird and Smiggle school sets at 2,200–4,300 EGP. We paid Google 2,274 EGP for “glossy bird” searches in thirty days and sent them to sold-out pages.

38.5M EGP of cash

Clothing holds 58% of the inventory and returns 0.95× on it

Clothing is 25% of revenue and 58% of the stock at cost, at a GMROI of 0.95× against 9.5× for School and 5.3× for Baby Essentials. It converts well online (1.86%, the best of the big lines) and earns 2.57 EGP a view — but only 13% of it is sold online. The cash is not trapped because the range is bad. It is trapped because the range is only being sold in seven rooms.

10.2% of online revenue

The online refund rate is double the in-store rate and nobody manages it

Online refunds and exchanges ran 10.2% in August and peaked at 14.6% last August, against 5.6% in store. That is roughly 950,000 EGP a month, and it is not in any margin number the team currently looks at. On a channel whose entire gross profit is 2.57M EGP a month, returns are a third of the remaining contribution.

Data integrity

Three systems, three different truths

Shopify says the store took 9.38M EGP gross in August; Odoo's Shopify sales team says 7.36M net of tax; GA4 records 9.09M of Egyptian purchase revenue. Product categories do not reconcile either — Odoo's “Various” is 994k of online revenue against 29k in GA4, because Shopify's product_type and Odoo's category are maintained separately. Every number in this deck is traceable, but no one of these systems can be handed to a board on its own. One reconciliation, once, before the next budget.

The action plan

Weeks 1–2 · no capital

Publish the range we already own and already sell

Push the 2,138 ACTIVE-but-unpublished Shopify products live, starting with the 1,960 SKUs that arrived in the last 120 days and are already doing 9.4M EGP a month in store. Then fix the 607 out-of-stock product pages: either restock, or redirect the traffic to the nearest in-stock sibling. Owner: e-commerce merchandising. Measure: listing coverage by in-store revenue, from 74.3% to 90%.

Weeks 2–6

Rebuild the cart-to-checkout step, then re-point the media

Instrument the cart and checkout on mobile — 98.6% of Egyptian traffic — and find the 85% loss. In parallel, move budget from Meta prospecting into Google Ads and into the categories with the highest profit per view, and stop buying search terms for products that are out of stock. Owner: e-commerce + performance. Measure: cart→checkout from 15.2% to 30%, GPPV from 3.42 back above 5.00.

Weeks 4–8

Prove or disprove the Meta offline halo with a geo-holdout

Four weeks, two matched governorates dark, measure total revenue including in-store. Meta needs to produce 4.2M EGP a month of incremental in-store revenue to justify its current budget. If it does, spend more. If it does not, the money moves. This is a four-week test that governs 90% of the media budget.

Quarter

Free the Clothing cash by selling Clothing online

Clothing already converts better than any other big line and is 90% listed. It is 13% online against a 14% company average and it holds 38.5M EGP. Give it its own traffic plan, its own creative and its own landing pages before ordering another season. Owner: category buying + e-commerce. Measure: Clothing GMROI from 0.95× to 1.5×.

What I need from you

Four decisions, this week

1. Do we publish everything we sell? Listing the in-store range online is free, reversible, and worth about 3.1M EGP a month. The only reason not to is stock accuracy. I need a yes and one owner.

2. Do we run the Meta geo-holdout? It costs four weeks of slightly lower spend in two governorates. Without it we are defending 2.15M EGP a month on faith.

3. Which number does the e-commerce team get judged on? I want it to be GPPV net of returns, not revenue and not ROAS. Revenue rewards buying traffic; ROAS rewards buying our own brand name back.

4. Who owns the reconciliation? Odoo, Shopify and GA4 disagree by up to 27%. One person, two weeks, one agreed definition of a sale — before the next budget is set on numbers we cannot defend.
